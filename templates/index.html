Research

My PhD research focuses on relativistic jets launched by quasars. Working with Łukasz Stawarz, I use models that go beyond the common homogeneous one-zone approximation: the jet is treated as a current-carrying, axially symmetric outflow in magnetohydrostatic equilibrium, whose pressure, toroidal magnetic field, and velocity-shear profiles are linked self-consistently.

In my recent first-author work I applied this framework to ten Chandra-resolved quasar jets at z ≥ 2.5. The Bayesian evidence favors radiating electrons that trace the gas pressure rather than the magnetic energy density, and points to jet powers reaching ~1049 erg s−1 — systematically higher than one-zone estimates suggest.

My second line of work concerns the VHE cosmology. In the newest preprint with Emmanuel Moulin we used public IACT data from STeVeCat to study the constraining power the role of systematic errors in the method of determination of cosmological parameters from blazar attenuation on EBL. In light of the Hubble tensions such methods are particularly interesting, as they are independent from both the cosmic distance ladder and the CMB determinations.

Finally, my professional work at IMGW-PIB in remote sensing focuses on data analysis, visualization and distribution as well as operational usage of satellites, at the moment mostly MTG FCI. I also help in any task that is short-staffed at the moment.
