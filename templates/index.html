Research

My PhD is on kiloparsec-scale quasar jets. With Łukasz Stawarz, I model their radio-to-X-ray spectra using synchrotron emission and inverse Compton scattering of the cosmic microwave background. We treat the jet as an axisymmetric, current-carrying outflow in magnetohydrostatic equilibrium. The model links the gas pressure, toroidal magnetic field and flow speed across the jet.

We fitted the model to ten quasar jets at z ≥ 2.5 with X-ray structure resolved by Chandra. The Bayesian evidence was highest when the density of radiating electrons followed the gas pressure. Our estimates of jet power reach about 1049 erg s−1 and are systematically higher than those from homogeneous one-zone models.

With Emmanuel Moulin, I use the attenuation of very-high-energy gamma rays from blazars to constrain the Hubble constant. We analysed archival spectra from STeVECat and found that the main uncertainty is how well we know the intensity of the extragalactic background light, which absorbs some of the gamma rays. The method is independent of the distance ladder and measurements based on the cosmic microwave background.

At IMGW-PIB, I analyse and visualise satellite data, mainly from the Flexible Combined Imager on Meteosat Third Generation. I also help distribute the data and use them in day-to-day operations.
